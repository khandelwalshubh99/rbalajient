That is a supply problem, not a pricing one, and it is the one worth solving.

Buyer's Guide: Choosing the Right Abrasive

The most-asked and worst-answered question in this trade. Cost per wheel is the wrong number; cost per kilogram of metal removed is the right one.

Bonded vs coated

Bonded abrasives (cut-off and grinding wheels) have grain held in a resin or vitrified bond through the full thickness, so they wear down and keep cutting. Coated abrasives (paper, discs, flap discs) have a single grain layer on a backing, so they cut fast and then stop. Bonded for stock removal and cutting; coated for surface, blending and finishing.

Grit by material and stage

Coarse grit (36–60) for stock removal and weld dressing; medium (80–120) for blending; fine (180+) for finish preparation. Going straight to fine to "save a step" removes nothing and glazes the disc: it is slower and more expensive than doing it in two stages.

Disc thickness against speed and life

A 1.0 mm cut-off wheel cuts faster, wanders less on thin section and generates less heat, but it is fragile and gives fewer cuts on heavy stock. 1.6–3.0 mm lasts longer on structural section. Matching thickness to section thickness is worth more than any brand argument.

Flap disc or grinding wheel for weld dressing

A grinding wheel removes a weld cap fast and leaves gouging to blend out. A flap disc removes slower but leaves a surface you can paint. On thin sheet, the wheel will burn through before it stops; use the flap disc. On heavy structural, use the wheel first and finish with the disc.

Why the cheapest wheel is usually the most expensive

A poor wheel loads up, glazes, and needs more pressure, which slows the cut, heats the work and shortens the grinder's life alongside its own. Track wheels-per-metre-cut for two weeks across two brands and the answer stops being an opinion.

Storage and shelf life

Bonded wheels absorb moisture and lose strength. Store flat, dry, off the floor, and use the oldest stock first. A wheel that has spent a monsoon in a damp corner is not a saving.

Safety: RPM, guard, ring test

The maximum RPM marked on the wheel must be equal to or higher than the tool's no-load speed, always, without exception. Keep the guard on. Ring-test bonded wheels before mounting: tap the edge, listen for a clear ring, and discard anything that thuds. These three checks cost nothing and prevent the one accident in this trade that is never minor.
