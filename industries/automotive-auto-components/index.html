Automotive & Auto Components

Line-side tooling, torque control and breakdown cover for OEMs and tier suppliers.

Operating Reality

Line stoppage in an assembly plant is measured in rupees per minute. That single fact governs everything about how tooling gets bought here: depth over breadth, standardisation over shopping around, and availability over a marginally better price.

Fasteners are torque-controlled and audited, which makes a torque wrench a measuring instrument rather than a spanner. Tooling is standardised across stations, so substituting a brand mid-year creates a problem, not a saving: kits stop being interchangeable and audit trails stop matching.

Below the OEMs sits the tier-2 and tier-3 ecosystem (press shops, forging units, machine shops, gear and transmission component makers, rubber and plastic component suppliers), and below that the aftermarket: authorised service workshops, fleet garages and transport operators along the Agra–Bombay road. The tool list changes at every level. The consequence of running out does not.

Buyer's Guide: How to Size a Bearing Puller

The most common avoidable damage we get called about is a scored shaft. It is almost always a sizing problem, not a technique problem.

Leg span

Measure across the back face of the bearing you have to grip, not the shaft. The puller has to close inside the housing clearance and still get behind the inner or outer race, whichever you are pulling against. A puller whose minimum span is larger than the clearance simply cannot be used, however much tonnage it has.

Reach

Reach is the distance from the leg tip to the screw centreline. If the bearing sits deep on a stepped shaft, a short-reach puller will pull at an angle, which is exactly how legs slip and shafts get gouged. Measure from the bearing face to the shaft end before ordering.

Tonnage

Sized on interference fit and bearing size, not on how stuck it feels. Under-tonnage means you lean on the bar and the legs deflect; over-tonnage is harmless but bulky. If you don't know, tell us the bearing number and we'll work it back.

Jaw thickness against bearing clearance

Thin jaws get behind tight races but bend under load; thick jaws are stronger but won't fit. This is the trade-off a two-leg and three-leg pair exists to solve; most plants need both, in two spans.

Two-leg or three-leg

Three-leg self-centres and is kinder to the shaft, and is the default for anything you can get all the way around. Two-leg is for when a housing, a keyway or an adjacent component blocks one side.

Send the bearing number and a photo of how it sits. We'd rather size it with you than sell you a puller that comes back.

Common Failure Points

Symptom, root cause, and the fix: the conversations we have most often on this shop floor.

Symptom

Fastener heads rounding on the line

Cause

Worn sockets, wrong drive size, or a 12-point socket used on an impact gun

Fix

Move to 6-point impact-rated sockets and keep hand sets and impact sets physically separate

Impact Socket · Deep Impact Socket
Symptom

Shaft scored during bearing removal

Cause

Puller span mismatched to the bearing, or the hammer-and-chisel method

Fix

Correct 2-leg / 3-leg sizing: see the span selection guide above

Bearing Puller · Gear Puller
Symptom

Torque wrench readings drifting

Cause

No recalibration schedule, and wrenches stored wound up at their working setting

Fix

Annual recalibration, and store every click wrench wound back to the lowest scale mark

Torque Wrench · Digital Torque Wrench
Symptom

Grinder failing every few months

Cause

DIY-grade tool put on production duty

Fix

Duty-cycle matched professional tool with restart protection and overload cut-out

Symptom
